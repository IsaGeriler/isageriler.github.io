Path Tracer & Light Transport Renderer

C++ · Multithreading · Intel Open Image Denoise · University of Warwick · 2026

Extended a CPU path tracer in C++ with light tracing, instant radiosity, and material models for metal, glass and layered surfaces to explore light transport and material behaviour.

Why it matters

Simulates highly realistic lighting and materials using industry-standard math and data structures, dramatically reducing render times and visual noise through spatial optimizations and Intel AI denoising.

  • Architecture: Three integrators in one engine: Path Tracing, Light Tracing, and Instant Radiosity (which uses a Halton quasi-Monte Carlo sampler relying on prime bases for the Radical Inverse).
  • Material framework: GGX microfacets (Conductor BSDF, sampled proportionally to the NDF), Plastic (Phong), Diffuse, Oren-Nayar, Glass, Mirror, and a Layered BSDF evaluating Beer's Law for attenuation.
  • Performance: Rendering distributed across CPU threads in 32 × 32 tiles, with a Binned SAH BVH (surface area heuristic bounding volume hierarchy) to reduce geometry searches and Multiple Importance Sampling (MIS) for latitude-longitude environment maps.
  • Denoising: Integrated Intel Open Image Denoise using colour, albedo and normal buffers, producing denoised images at 16 samples per pixel across four test scenes.

Software Rasterizer Optimisation

C++ · SIMD · Multithreading · University of Warwick · 2026

Optimised a supplied CPU rasterizer by profiling bottlenecks, applying SIMD instructions, and caching transformed vertices to avoid repeated calculations. Sequential optimisations cut mean execution time by 49.3%: the 1,600-object benchmark scene fell from 86.4 s to 43.8 s across 10 runs on an Intel Core Ultra 7 155H. I also evaluated a thread pool across three test scenes to investigate CPU scaling.

Hardware profiling · the "thread plateau"

I profiled how performance scales with thread count on my Intel Core Ultra 7 155H. In the Scene 3 results below, speedup peaks at roughly 2.8× around 6 threads, then degrades. This thread-scaling experiment is separate from the sequential optimisation benchmark above; thread count alone does not establish the cause of the plateau.

Frontrooms

Individual game · Unreal Engine 5 · C++ / HLSL / Blueprints · University of Warwick · 2026

A stealth-horror game whose core mechanic is a denoiser. Enemies are signed distance fields sphere-traced in HLSL and hidden under visual noise. Holding still lets the image resolve and reveals where they are, but holding still is also what lets them close the distance.

Why it matters

It began as a critical literature review of SDF rendering and denoising, and the research became the design rather than a footnote to it. Sample convergence is normally invisible plumbing; here the convergence rate is the risk/reward curve the player is reading. Same territory as the Intel OIDN work in 001, turned into a mechanic instead of a post-process.

  • Enemy rendering: enemies are signed distance fields sphere-traced in an HLSL shader, each with independently configurable appearance parameters, so a new silhouette is authored as SDF parameters rather than as a mesh.
  • The mechanic: screen-space noise resolves while the player holds still, tying denoise convergence directly to enemy visibility and to how much ground the enemy gains while you wait.
  • AI: enemy vision, pursuit and retreat written in C++, with debug draw visualization used to verify detection state during development.
  • Iteration: built in the open with fortnightly demos to staff and peers. Movement speeds were retuned and a tutorial added directly off the back of playtest feedback.

Skyfire Uprising · Level 2

Team game · Unreal Engine 5.4 · Tech Art & Group Lead · University of Warwick · 2026

Led a six-person group responsible for Level 2 within a 42-person international team split across seven level teams. I coordinated the group's work and kept the level consistent with the shared art direction while developing its weather, materials, lighting, and damage-feedback post-process stack.

Shipping inside a 42-person studio

Seven groups each owned a level against one shared art direction, so anything I wrote had to survive other people's hands. Every material exposed its parameters upward, letting teammates tune rain intensity, wetness and fog against their own set dressing without opening a shader graph or coming back to me.

  • Weather & materials: Gerstner wave water, a Niagara rain system with splashes and puddle accumulation, and a reusable wetness layer that darkens albedo and drops roughness across the level's surfaces.
  • Lighting & atmosphere: volumetric fog tuned across the whole level footprint, and interior lighting passes for the warehouse spaces the level routes players through.
  • Damage feedback: a full post-process state stack — a red screen-space response on taking damage, a chromatic-aberration and slice-displacement glitch on death, and a grid overlay marking respawn invulnerability.
  • Performance: Cull Distance Volumes used to scale the level's draw load across a large outdoor footprint.
